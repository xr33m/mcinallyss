Common Causes

What Causes a Burst or Frozen Pipe?

Most pipe emergencies come down to a few causes.

Freezing and Thaw Splits

Water expands as it freezes and can split a pipe, which then leaks as it thaws.

Failed Joints and Fittings

Push-fit joints, compression fittings and flexible hoses can fail without warning.

Corrosion and Age

Old pipework can corrode through at joints and bends.

Pressure and Movement

Pipes can be knocked, stressed or damaged during other work.

Burst and Frozen Pipes: What to Know

Know your stopcock

Find it now and check it turns. In many homes it is under the kitchen sink, and in some tenement buildings the supply is shared. Knowing where it is saves minutes when it matters.

Thawing a frozen pipe

Open the nearest tap, then warm the pipe gently with a hairdryer on a low setting or warm towels, starting nearest the tap. Never use a blowtorch, a naked flame or boiling water. Check for leaks as it thaws.

Who is responsible

Scottish Water generally looks after the public main up to the property boundary, and the owner is responsible for the supply pipe within it. If you are not sure, Scottish Water can advise.

Preventing a repeat

Lag pipes in lofts and outhouses and on external walls, fix dripping taps, and keep the heating on low in very cold weather if you are away. See our leak repair page for slower leaks.
