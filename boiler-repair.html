When to Call

Boiler Faults We Repair Across Edinburgh

These are the boiler problems Edinburgh homeowners and landlords most often call us about.

Boiler Showing a Fault Code

Tell us the code and the make and model. It is often the fastest route to the cause.

Boiler Losing Pressure

Needing to top up the pressure regularly usually means a leak or a faulty expansion vessel. See our guide to why boilers lose pressure.

No Hot Water

Heating works but no hot water, or the other way round? That usually points to a diverter valve, pump or controls fault.

Boiler Making Strange Noises

Banging, kettling or whistling can mean limescale, trapped air or poor flow. Worth fixing before it becomes a breakdown.

Radiators Cold or Slow to Heat

Cold radiators can be air, sludge or a failing pump or valve. We work out whether it is the boiler or the system.

Pilot or Ignition Not Lighting

Ignition and sensor faults are common. We diagnose, repair and test the boiler before we leave.

Common Causes

What Usually Causes a Boiler Breakdown?

A fault code and a description of the symptoms usually point to one of these areas.

Low Water Pressure

A leak somewhere in the system or a failing expansion vessel. See our guide to boiler pressure loss.

Ignition and Sensor Faults

The boiler will not fire, or fires and shuts down. Often a sensor, electrode or gas-valve issue.

Circulation Problems

A failing pump, a stuck valve or sludge can stop heat getting where it is needed.

Frozen or Blocked Condensate

In freezing weather the condensate pipe can freeze and shut a condensing boiler down.

Boiler Breakdowns: What to Check Before You Call

Safety first

If you smell gas or suspect a carbon monoxide leak, turn off the boiler, open windows and call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999. Do not remove the boiler casing yourself. Gas work must be done by a Gas Safe registered engineer.

Quick checks

Check the display for a fault code and note it down, check the pressure gauge against your manual (many boilers want roughly 1 to 2 bar when cold, but check yours), and make sure the programmer, thermostat and any batteries are fine. If the boiler restarts after a reset and stops again, tell us.

In freezing weather

If a condensing boiler stops in a cold snap, the condensate pipe may have frozen. Pouring warm (never boiling) water over the frozen section is the commonly published first step, if you can reach it safely. If it keeps happening, the pipe may need rerouting or insulating.
